# Mia–Ava academic interpretation in production context exploration

## Purpose

Use academic lenses to make one practical distinction easier to perceive or one next action more intentional. This is a supporting practice of T5, not an academic detour, a new team, or an automatic episode pipeline. Mia can name the structural relations; Ava's imagery can make them approachable. When only one agent authors both voices, label them as proposed narration, not independent witnesses.

## Workflow

1. Preserve the supplied dialogue and exact prompt in the exploration's `.mia/` folder. Record origin and whether it is user-supplied, authored here, or an observed exchange.
2. Place corrections beside the original. Treat an uncertain voice-transcription token as unresolved, not as permission to invent a domain. Confirmed noise leaves the inquiry but remains in the historical quote.
3. Choose specific fields and at most two useful subfields per field. Avoid reducing everything to generic “AI” or “technology”. Useful lenses include knowledge representation/schema evolution, archival provenance, human–computer interaction/CSCW, episodic memory/distributed cognition, narrative studies, situated Indigenous methodologies, and practice-based organizational learning.
4. Honor the requested surface exactly. For the prompt below, start with `## Academics` followed by a blank line; use `### <main field>` and optional `#### <subfield>`, no introduction, no displayed word counts. Keep each main field's body, including its subfield paragraphs, under 55 words as a conservative reading. Count with a tool, excluding headings.
5. End the interpretive work by naming its practical implication in the ledger or script: what to preserve, link, verify, prototype, or leave human-held. Do not add that sentence to the academic surface if it breaks the requested format.
6. Keep a survey of lenses distinct from a literature review. If citations or scholarly claims are required, inspect the actual sources and record them; do not fabricate authority. Cultural specificity, participant consent, community protocols, and data sovereignty are not software metadata defaults.

## What this does not prove

Metaphor does not prove automatic storytelling. Dialogue does not prove two agents ran. An academic name does not prove the runtime implements its theory. A promising interpretation does not choose an episode. Preserve these limits while still producing a useful staged script.

## Verification

Read back the preserved input, corrections, academic surface, and actual script. Check heading shape, aggregate body words per field, at most two subfields, and no invented receipts. Ensure the interpretation changed a practical action rather than merely adding terminology.
